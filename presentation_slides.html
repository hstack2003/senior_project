Here Comes the Sun: The Effect of Earlier Sunrises and Sunsets on Academic Achievement

Hannah M. Stackpole

Literature TL;DR

  • DST moves a location one time zone father east
  • Later sunsets result in less sleep and more disease (Giuntella & Mazzona, 2019)
  • Later school starts (better for adolescents) result in higher grades, more time on HW, less time on TV, less day-time sleepiness, fewer depressive symptoms (Carrell, 2011; Edwards, 2012; Heissell & Norris, 2018; Wahlstrom, 2002; 2017)

Conceptual Model

  • Three clocks: sun, body, social (Roenneberg et al., 2019)
  • Sunsets & sleep timing: especially important for adolescents
  • Sleep & test scores
    • Less sleep (chronic sleep deprivation)
    • Later sunrises (drowsy in mornings)
  • Long term implications: human capital, wages, and life outcomes (Becker, 1964; Mincer, 1974; Card, 2000; Hanushek & Woessmann, 2008; 2012; Chetty et al., 2014; Mulligan et al., 1999; Murnane et al., 1995; 2000).
    • educational achievement matters (not just attainment)

Empirical Framework

  • Economists’ favorite: DiD
  • \({Y}_{itg} = \beta_1(\text{treated}_{ig} \times \text{post_treat}_t) + \alpha_i + \gamma_t + \delta_g + \varepsilon_{itg}\)
  • treatment counties move to Central time zone in April 2006
